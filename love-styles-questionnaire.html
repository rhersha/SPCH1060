Love Styles Questionnaire

This questionnaire explores your attitudes about romantic love, using the six love styles described by sociologist John Alan Lee. Rate how much you agree with each statement.

There are no right answers, and you don’t need to be in a relationship to take it. Answer based on what love means to you and how you tend to act when you’re in love, not on how any particular relationship is going.

Your answers stay in your browser. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Your love styles

Lee compared love styles to colors. Eros, Storge, and Ludus are the primaries. Each style between them is a blend of its two neighbors, the way orange is a blend of red and yellow. The longer a petal, the more you agreed with that style. The heart shows where your answers balance out.

Your love color

    Most people agree with several styles at once, and your profile can shift with time and with different relationships. This questionnaire is a tool for self-reflection, not a validated research instrument or a judgment about you or your relationship.

    Show how each score was calculated

    Each style has four statements. Your score for a style is the average of your four ratings, from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

      Statements written for this course, based on the love styles described in: Lee, J. A. (1977). A typology of styles of loving. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 3(2), 173–182.

      For the validated research measure of love styles, see: Hendrick, C., & Hendrick, S. S. (1986). A theory and method of love. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 50(2), 392–402.
